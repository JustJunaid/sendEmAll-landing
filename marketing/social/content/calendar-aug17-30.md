### Wed Aug 19 | 10 AM ET | Deliverability Story | Trigger: Fear 🔶
```
I watched a client burn a 14-month-old domain in 9 days.

Not with spam. With a "clean" list they bought from a reputable-sounding vendor.

Here's what actually happened:

Day 1-3: campaign starts. 7% of emails bounce. Nobody notices — the dashboard shows "delivered: 93%," and that sounds fine.

Day 4-6: Google notices. A domain that suddenly hits thousands of dead mailboxes looks exactly like a spammer warming up. Open rates quietly halve.

Day 7-9: even replies to REAL conversations start landing in spam. The domain is cooked — including for the deals already in flight.

The lesson that stuck with me: deliverability doesn't fail loudly. It fails silently, while your dashboard says everything's fine.

The three numbers I now check before any list goes near a sending domain:

→ Bounce rate on a test slice — over 3% and the list doesn't get sent. Period.
→ Catch-all percentage — these "deliver" but tell you nothing. They get their own careful lane.
→ How old the data is — people change jobs every 18-24 months. A 6-month-old list is a coin flip.

A burned domain takes months to rebuild. Checking those three numbers takes an afternoon.

Have you ever lost a domain to a bad list? What did it cost you? 👇
```

```
"How do you decide who makes the list?"

I get this question more than any other. Here's my honest answer — the signals I check before a company earns a cold email:

1. Are they hiring for the role my client sells to (or the role that uses the product)? A company posting 3 SDR jobs has an outbound problem TODAY. Not someday.

2. Did money just show up? Fresh funding means budgets get spent in the next 2-3 quarters. Timing is everything.

3. Did their stack change? New CRM, new sales tools — that's a team actively rebuilding how they sell. Doors are open.

4. Is leadership talking about the problem publicly? A VP posting about pipeline pain is telling you what email to write.

5. Is the signal FRESH? Last week's signal is gold. Last quarter's is trivia. I'd rather have 50 companies with this-month signals than 5,000 without.

The pattern behind all five: I'm not asking "does this company match a filter?" I'm asking "is there a reason to talk to them right now?"

Title-and-industry filters answer the first question. Only signals answer the second.

And the second question is the only one buyers can feel when they open your email.

Operators — what's on your signal checklist that I missed? Building this list in public, genuinely want to know 👇
```

### Fri Aug 28 | 10 AM ET | Operations: Speed to Reply | Trigger: Curiosity
```
Cold email teams obsess over getting replies. Almost nobody talks about what happens AFTER the reply.

Here's what changed how I run campaigns: reply to an interested prospect within the hour and your odds of booking the meeting are dramatically higher than replying tomorrow. Interest decays like ice in the sun.

And yet — the standard setup practically guarantees slow responses:

→ Replies land in 12 different mailboxes across 4 sending domains
→ Nobody's actually logged into inbox #9 since setup
→ The "interested!" reply sits for 3 days next to warmup emails and bounce notices
→ By the time someone answers, the prospect's priority moved on

Teams spend $500/mo generating replies they then... leave on the table.

The fixes that actually work, cheapest first:

1. One unified inbox for every sending mailbox. Replies from all domains, one screen. Non-negotiable.
2. Notifications for positive replies only (not every auto-responder). Signal-to-noise matters or you'll stop checking.
3. A same-day reply rule during business hours. Not a template — a human answering while the interest is real.

The uncomfortable summary: your reply rate is a vanity metric if your response time is measured in days.

How fast does your team actually respond to an interested reply? Be honest — when did you last audit it? 👇
```
