### Mon Apr 14 | 10 AM ET | Framework: Educational/Tactical | Trigger: Curiosity
**Tag:** `#DNS` `#infrastructure`

```
I set up DNS records for cold email every week.

80% of the time, the previous setup has at least one of these wrong:

𝗦𝗣𝗙 (Sender Policy Framework)
What it does: Tells email providers which servers can send from your domain.
Common mistake: Having multiple SPF records. You can only have ONE per domain. Multiple = both break.

𝗗𝗞𝗜𝗠 (DomainKeys Identified Mail)
What it does: Adds a cryptographic signature proving your emails weren't altered.
Common mistake: Setting up DKIM for your primary domain but not your sending domains. Each domain needs its own.

𝗗𝗠𝗔𝗥𝗖 (Domain-based Message Authentication)
What it does: Tells providers what to do when SPF or DKIM fails.
Common mistake: Not having one at all. Or setting p=none and forgetting about it forever.

𝗖𝘂𝘀𝘁𝗼𝗺 𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗰𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗱𝗼𝗺𝗮𝗶𝗻
What it does: Routes click tracking through YOUR domain instead of your tool's shared one.
Common mistake: Using Instantly's or Lemlist's default tracking domain. Shared with thousands of senders. One bad actor = your emails suffer.

If you're sending cold emails without all four configured correctly, you're leaving replies on the table.

How's your DNS setup? Drop a screenshot of your MXToolbox results and I'll tell you what needs fixing 👇

### Mon Apr 21 | 10 AM ET | Framework: Data/Educational | Trigger: Curiosity
**Tag:** `#data` `#openrate`

```
Your 55% open rate is lying to you.

Here's what most outbound teams don't know:

Open tracking works by embedding a tiny invisible image in your email. When the recipient's email client loads that image, it counts as an "open."

The problem:

1️⃣ Apple Mail Privacy Protection pre-loads ALL images automatically. Your email shows as "opened" even if nobody read it. ~50% of email users are on Apple Mail.

2️⃣ Corporate email security tools scan links and images before delivering. Another false "open."
